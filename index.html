Privacy Policy

Spy Detector for Android · package com.techs4arab.spydetector · version 1.0 · last updated 15 November 2026

Summary

Scanning happens entirely on your device. The app has no internet permission and sends nothing to anyone. There is no account, no advertising, no analytics and no crash reporting.

Who publishes the app

Spy Detector is developed and published by Mohamed Elsamman. Contact: mohamed.elsamman.dev@gmail.com.

What the app reads when you scan

When you start a scan, the app reads from Android:

  • the list of installed apps: name, package ID, version and install source;
  • the permissions each app declares and has been granted;
  • the fingerprints of each app's signing certificates;
  • which apps have an accessibility service, notification access or device admin switched on;
  • whether drawing over other apps and usage access are allowed for each app;
  • a few phone settings: screen lock, security-patch date, USB debugging, developer options, root indicators, encryption and Android version.

The app itself does not request camera, microphone, location, SMS, contacts or call-log permissions. A scan runs only when you start it; there is no background monitoring.

What is stored on your device

The app stores the latest scan report (the information listed above), the list of apps you marked “trusted” (package name and signing fingerprint), and your theme and language choices. This data is kept in the app's private storage, is excluded from Android backup and device-to-device transfer, and is not encrypted by the app itself. A new scan replaces the report; no scan history is kept.

What leaves your device

Nothing. This version has no internet permission and no network code, so the developer does not receive your data and nothing is shared with third parties. When you tap “Remove app” or “Open settings”, Android's own screens take over and are governed by Android's and your device maker's policies.

Permissions the app requests

  • QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES (“See all installed apps”): needed so the scan can compare every installed app, including apps whose names it cannot know in advance, with a list of known stalkerware indicators.
  • REQUEST_DELETE_PACKAGES (“Request to delete packages”): lets the “Remove app” button open Android's own removal confirmation. The app never removes anything without your confirmation.

No dangerous permission is requested at run time. The app does not declare the internet permission.

Third-party components

The app uses the AndroidX, Jetpack Compose and Kotlin Coroutines libraries to run its interface and scan; none of them sends data from the app. The known-indicator list is a file bundled inside the app and is not updated over the internet. It is derived from the Echap stalkerware-indicators project, licensed under CC BY 4.0, converted to a compact format.

What the scan can and cannot tell you

A clean result means no match was found in the bundled indicator list among the apps the scan could inspect. It does not mean the phone is free of spyware: new, modified or unlisted spyware may not be detected, and a granted permission is not proof that an app used it. A match on a name or certificate can be a false alarm, so check where an app came from before removing it.

Your choices and deleting your data

You can delete the saved report and the trusted-apps list at any time from the app (Privacy & about → “Delete saved results and trusted apps”, which keeps your theme and language choices), with Android's Settings → Apps → Spy Detector → Storage → Clear storage, or by uninstalling the app. Because the developer does not receive your data, there is no copy held by the developer to access or erase.

Children

The app collects no personal information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If the way data is handled changes (for example by adding an internet connection), this policy will be updated before the change takes effect, and the “last updated” date above will change.
